      From free data to a real business

      AeroVita is an entrepreneurship (PKWU) case study: how a free, open API becomes a product with a defensible unique selling proposition.

      01 · Problem

      Weather apps tell you the temperature. They rarely tell you what to do.

      Farmers still irrigate by habit. Runners still train in unhealthy air. Travellers still arrive in monsoon season. The data exists — the interpretation doesn't.

      02 · Solution

      A decision layer on top of open data.

      We ingest free weather + air-quality feeds and translate them into prescriptive, human-readable actions for health and agriculture — no sensors, no hardware, no database.

      03 · Unique Selling Proposition

      Zero-cost data stack

      Open-Meteo needs no API key and no billing account, so gross margin starts near 100%.

      Prescriptive, not descriptive

      Competitors show numbers. We show "irrigate tonight" or "don't run outside at 3pm".

      Dual-market by design

      One engine, two buyers: personal health and precision agriculture.

      Local-first

      City-level granularity with local language and local crops — hard for global apps to match.

      04 · Market segments
      • Eco-tourism — advise visitors on the best window to hike, dive or trek based on air quality and rainfall.
      • Precision agriculture — smallholder co-ops and agronomists needing irrigation and spraying windows.
      • Urban health — schools, outdoor sports clubs and respiratory patients.
      05 · Revenue model
      • Freemium — free city dashboard, paid field-level alerts via WhatsApp.
      • B2B API — white-label advisory feed for agri-tech and travel platforms.
      • Sponsorship — agri-input brands sponsoring seasonal recommendations.
      06 · Competitive advantage

      The moat is not the data — anyone can call the same API. The moat is the recommendation ruleset: crop-specific thresholds, local agronomic knowledge and health guidelines tuned per region. Every new region we calibrate raises switching costs and improves accuracy, creating a compounding data flywheel without owning a single sensor.
